About ERA ERA is a free, community-curated web resource for the biological and clinical interpretation of epigenetic reprogramming and its effects on aging. Partial reprogramming, chemical cocktails, and epigenome editing can reset aspects of cellular age, and the literature describing which interventions do what — and under which conditions — is growing rapidly. Today that knowledge sits scattered across papers and private notes, so effort is endlessly repeated. ERA centralizes it: structured, versioned evidence that anyone can read and that the community reviews together.

ERA is an open access, open source, community-driven knowledgebase for the Epigenetic Reprogramming Atlas for Longevity. Our goal is to centralize, debate, and curate the evidence linking reprogramming factors, chemical cocktails, delivery strategies, and epigenetic marks to rejuvenation, lifespan, and healthspan outcomes — so that this knowledge is developed in the open rather than repeatedly rebuilt inside private databases.

All content created in ERA is, and will continue to be, freely available, without restriction, under a Public Domain (CC0) License. Anyone can contribute by registering for an account. Users join the community with “Curator” level privileges that allow them to comment on or propose additions and revisions to any content in ERA. Proposed additions and revisions are then reviewed by users with “Editor” or “Admin” level privileges before gaining “Accepted” status. Content that has been submitted but not yet accepted should be interpreted and used with caution. Community Curators work together with Editors towards comprehensive and accurate interpretations.

ERA is an independent, unfunded, volunteer-run project. It is not affiliated with any company or institution, and curated content will always remain free to access anonymously, without login, with no fees introduced.

We are looking for contributors — aging and epigenetics researchers to help design the evidence model and curate the first entries, curators to enter structured evidence from the reprogramming literature, and developers (Rails / GraphQL / Angular) to help build the platform. ERA is a fork of CIViC (Clinical Interpretation of Variants in Cancer), developed by the Griffith Lab at the McDonnell Genome Institute, Washington University School of Medicine. We are grateful for their work and for releasing both the data and the platform openly. ERA reuses CIViC's curation and moderation model and adapts the domain from cancer variants to epigenetic reprogramming and longevity.

If you use ERA, please also acknowledge and cite the original CIViC publication: Griffith, M., Spies, N., Krysiak, K. et al. CIViC is a community knowledgebase for expert crowdsourcing the clinical interpretation of variants in cancer. Nat Genet 49, 170–174 (2017).
